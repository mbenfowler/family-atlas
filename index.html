Two views of the same tree: how well each ancestor is proven, and where each generation was born. Built from the FamilySearch snapshot of 17 November 2026, compared with the family's Ancestry tree as of 19 November 2026. You and your parents are shown by relationship only.

How far back the tree is proven

  • Strong. Every step back to you is tied by a census household or a shared record.
  • Fair. The people are documented, but one step has no record tying parent to child.
  • Weak. One step rests on online trees alone.
  • Contradicted. The evidence in hand points against a step, through records or dates that cannot fit. New evidence could reverse it, so each one carries an estimated chance it is still right.
  • Unknown. No parent recorded.
  • 🇫🇷Flags. Born outside the United States, per the tree. In the two outer rings the wedges are too thin, so the flag sits on the rim in the same direction.
  • Dots. An open dot marks a place where the family's Ancestry tree names a different person; a solid dot marks a person found only in the Ancestry tree. Select the wedge to see the difference.
Hover or tap a wedge to see who it is and why it has that grade. Rings run from your parents at the centre out to your 7th-great-grandparents. Your father's side is on the left, your mother's on the right.
Counts by generation
Contradicted links, and the chance each is still right

The percentages are judgment calls, not calculations: a rough estimate of how likely the link is to survive if the original records were checked. A link contradicted only by dates scores higher when a small error in one date would fix it.

Grades are computed from the records attached in FamilySearch, plus the links checked by hand this week. Before 1850 the census did not list household members, so older links can rarely rate better than Fair without a will, deed or Bible record.

Where each generation was born

Each dot is a birthplace; its area shows how many direct ancestors of that generation were born there. US places are plotted by county where the tree gives one, otherwise by state; places abroad are plotted by country. The map shows the eastern United States and western Europe on separate panels at different scales. Each thin arrow runs from a parent's birthplace (pale dot) to their child's birthplace in the selected generation, and is drawn only when the two differ; thicker arrows carry more families. Hover a dot for the people born there, or an arrow for who moved; click either to list them all beside the map. Faint dots show the generation before. Generations beyond your 4th-great-grandparents come mostly from shared online trees and are unverified.
